# SOMEWHAT INTELLIGENT + FRIEND / mixed-shirt campaign

This study places the `SOMEWHAT / INTELLIGENT` and `I THINK WE SHOULD BE
FRIENDS` objects inside the same campaign world. The people read as friends,
collaborators, or co-conspirators rather than a couple: independent posture,
physical space, shared work, and no romance-coded contact.

The three frames establish a useful product grammar:

1. `01-sky-floor-parking-garage-4x3.png` — both fronts, fully readable, on two
   separate figures in an automated garage. The flooded floor reflects a noon
   sky that cannot exist beneath the concrete ceiling.
2. `02-moon-wafer-cleanroom-4x3.png` — SOMEWHAT front plus FRIEND documentation
   back in a semiconductor cleanroom. Ceiling carriers transport small full
   moons instead of wafers.
3. `03-surveillance-fish-network-floor-4x3.png` — both fronts visible while the
   pair assemble an unauthorized network on the control-room floor. One fish
   swims continuously across hundreds of surveillance displays.

## Relationship direction

Keep the chemistry socially ambiguous and cooler than the preceding soft-glare
study. Subjects may share a task, room, risk, joke, or system, but should not
embrace, cuddle, kiss, lean heads together, or perform mirrored couple poses.
The appeal comes from confidence, competence, and the suggestion of shared
access.

## Image direction

- one impossible fact, photographed without spectacle;
- tack-sharp people, garment fronts, hands, and print;
- washed institutional glare, halation, fine film grain, shallow focus, and
  selective environmental motion blur;
- contemporary privately governed technology rather than generic cyberpunk;
- no police cosplay, weapons, protest-sign shorthand, neon HUDs, or fantasy
  effects.
