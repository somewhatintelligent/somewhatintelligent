# SOMEWHAT INTELLIGENT / soft-glare surreal campaign

This study makes the existing surreal direction cuter, softer, hotter, and
more openly intimate without giving up its institutional hostility. Each frame
places tack-sharp adult subjects inside washed, blurred infrastructure built to
classify, compute, surveil, or optimize them.

The visual contradiction is the system:

- hard apparatus / soft bodies;
- administrative control / private care;
- sharp flash / milky environmental glare;
- one legible impossibility / otherwise documentary material realism.

## Frames

1. `01-biometric-meadow-4x3.png` — two adults curl together in a security tray
   while the biometric scanner's conveyor quietly emerges as clover.
2. `02-ocean-server-bedroom-4x3.png` — an intimate manicure on a white bed in a
   cold aisle; each server rack contains a moonlit ocean horizon.
3. `03-satellite-dish-pool-4x3.png` — two adults lounge inside a steeply tilted
   rooftop satellite dish whose level water ignores gravity.
4. `04-reverse-rain-fulfillment-floor-4x3.png` — two adults stand forehead to
   forehead beneath an emergency shower while water moves upward and autonomous
   warehouse robots smear past them.

## Photographic rule

Treat the people, their contact, cotton, skin, hands, and shirt typography as
the sharp plane. Let the coercive environment recede through pale overexposure,
fluorescent halation, creamy veiling flare, shallow focus, chemical color fade,
and dragged-shutter motion blur. The result should feel like late-1990s radical
fashion editorial photography, not contemporary luxury advertising or
cyberpunk concept art.

## Surreal rule

Use one impossible fact per image and photograph it without spectacle. Do not
add portals, magic light, fantasy color, melting objects, generalized dream
haze, protest-sign shorthand, or fetishized police imagery. The political
charge comes from tenderness occupying infrastructure designed for control.

## Garment boundary

The image-generation artwork reference is the canonical centered two-line
wordmark. The production screen-print field remains exactly `16in wide × 18in
high`; the generated photographs are art-direction studies rather than
dimensional print proofs.
